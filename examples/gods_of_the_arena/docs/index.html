THE MATCH

How the arena works

Gods of the Arena is a 5v5 lane battler. Red brings one drafted roster and Blue another. Each side starts with five heroes, six barracks, nine lane towers, two god guards, and a god to defend.

The 116 by 116 arena uses the map editor's saved seed 54 preset. Blue starts in the southwest and Red in the northeast. Roads carve the forest and cross cliffs at short ramps. Each fort has three exits, two barracks per exit, and a separate walled spawn. Match seeds control gameplay randomness without changing this layout.

Each barracks spawns three melee creeps and one ranged caster every twenty seconds: six melee and two ranged creeps per lane for each team. They march until they meet the enemy. Heroes start at level 1 with 150 gold and their four abilities locked and one point to spend. Kills grant gold and XP. Each hero level grants another ability point, up to level 20. Gold buys items for a six-slot inventory.

Outer towers fall first, then inner, then the gate. Clearing a lane exposes the two level-3 towers guarding that team's god. Both guards must fall before the god can take any damage. Destroying a tower or barracks grants its killer 200 XP. Red defends Hades and Blue defends Zeus. The first god to reach zero health loses. Killing the enemy god grants every hero on your team a flat 1,000 XP, including dead or distant heroes. The reward is included in lifetime XP and the final score.

THE ROSTER

Heroes and ability kits

Each ability shows what it affects beside its artwork. Footprints describe the hit area; visual effects describe its appearance. Arc and cone effects share a sector footprint. Every area spell leaves a warning on the map until impact.

Hero levels and spell progression

Heroes start at level 1 with one ability point and all four spells locked at rank 0. Each hero level grants another point, up to level 20. Spend one point to unlock a spell at rank 1 or to raise it by one rank. Points stay banked until you choose to spend them.

Choose each upgrade: press Shift + Q/W/E/R, Shift-click an ability, or click its gold + button. BASIC bots use levelAbility(slot). Reaching the required hero level does not unlock or upgrade a spell automatically. Fully ranking all four spells costs 15 points.

Hero stats grow automatically. Basic-attack damage scales as base damage + (hero level − 1) × damage per level, plus equipment. Spell damage, healing, and mana restoration grow only with spell rank: 100%, 150%, 200%, then 250% of rank 1, rounded down. Mana costs, charges, cooldowns, and ranges stay the same across ranks. THE SHOP

Items, costs, and bonuses

Six inventory slots. Consumables stack to 8 and show their count on the icon. Heroes start with 150 gold. Purchases require being inside your own keep, including its spawn room. Footmen drop 15, towers 75, heroes 100.

Consumables

Health Potion

30

Use: restore 120 health over 10 seconds. Damage interrupts regeneration.

Vitality Elixir

75

Use: restore 90 health instantly.

Mana Potion

45

Use: restore 90 mana over 10 seconds. Damage interrupts regeneration.

Mana Elixir

90

Use: restore 60 mana instantly.

Poison Potion

40

Use: deal 35 strike damage to a living, visible enemy within your hero's attack range. Tower order and god protection still apply.

Health potions and elixirs share a 10-second cooldown. Mana potions and elixirs share a separate 10-second cooldown. Cooldowns begin on use and survive damage and death. Both slow regeneration effects stop on any positive incoming damage; moving and attacking do not interrupt them. Rejected uses consume nothing.

BASIC: item IDs are 1 Health Potion, 2 Vitality Elixir, 22 Mana Potion, and 3 Mana Elixir. itemCooldown(slot) reports live remaining ticks (24 per second), or zero for an empty/invalid slot. canShop() reports whether purchases are allowed. Buying elsewhere returns ActionOutsideKeep.

Inside your own spawn room, health and mana each recover at 20% of maximum per second. This is separate from potions and continues under attack. It does not apply elsewhere in the keep or in the enemy spawn, and cannot revive a dead hero. inOwnSpawn() reports whether your living hero is in this recovery area.

Portal Scroll

Item 21 costs 100 gold and stacks to eight. Click the scroll, then right-click the map or minimap. It consumes one scroll and channels for 3 seconds, teleporting to visible, walkable ground within a living allied tower's sight (9 tiles for outer towers, 9.5 for inner towers, 10 for gate/guard towers, matching attack range). Distant destinations are clamped into tower range. Barracks cannot anchor a teleport.

You cannot move, attack, or cast during the channel, but can still take damage and die. Stun, root, death, or destruction of the selected tower cancels it. Damage alone does not cancel it. Completion or interruption starts a shared 60-second cooldown, preserved through death. Blazing Blade, Golem Seed, and Bone Marionette can interrupt the channel on impact. Silence does not interrupt it.

Control effects: stun blocks movement, basic attacks, abilities, and items, cancelling a pending swing. Silence blocks all four abilities but allows moving, attacking, and items. Root blocks moving and teleporting but allows in-range attacks, abilities, and other items. Stun and root interrupt teleport channels. Already released spells still resolve. Effects apply to enemy heroes and creeps; buildings and gods are immune. Different effects coexist, with icons and countdown rings above visible units. Reapplying one keeps the later expiration instead of adding durations. Death clears all effects. Silenced casts return ActionSilenced (35).

BASIC: useItemAt(slot, x, y) accepts fractional coordinates and returns 1 on success, 0 on rejection. selfPortalCooldown, selfChannelTicks, selfStunTicks, selfRootTicks, and selfSilenceTicks report remaining ticks. useItem(slot) rejects scrolls because they require a destination.

BEFORE THE BATTLE

Draft your team

A seeded random team picks first. Teams then alternate, with each team's players picking in spawn order. All ten heroes share one pool, and each can be picked once. Combat, waves, and the battle clock wait for every player to pick.

In player mode, select an available hero and click Lock in hero on your turn. The active player appears above the hero grid. Picked heroes turn gray and show their player and team. Space pauses or resumes drafting.

Every player, including humans, has ten simulation seconds to pick. The countdown pauses with the game. If the deadline expires, the game picks a random available hero. Drafting takes at most 100 simulation seconds for ten players and does not consume battle time: maxTicks and the CLI duration flags start counting after the last pick.

BASIC scripts read drafting and draftTurnId, then call draftHero(class). Only the active player's script runs during drafting, every half second. The bundled base and rusher scripts try to fill frontline, carry, mage, support, and fighter roles. The draft itself allows any combination of available heroes, including multiple supports or carries.

Use draftPlayerCount(), draftPlayerId(i), and draftPlayerTeam(i) to inspect the public roster in spawn order. draftedClass(playerId) returns the selected class or -1 if unpicked. heroAvailable(class) checks the shared pool. heroRole(class) returns frontline 0, carry 1, mage 2, support 3, or fighter 4.

draftHero returns 1 on success, 0 on rejection. lastActionError() reports ActionNotDrafting, ActionNotDraftTurn, ActionUnknownHero, or ActionHeroTaken. Normal commands report ActionDrafting until drafting finishes. selfClass is -1 before your pick. worldTick includes drafting for replay timing.

ACTIONS AND SPELLS

Aim, cast, impact, recharge

Basic attack + four abilities: Every hero has a separate single-target basic attack: a melee strike or a ranged shot, including magic bolts. Basic attacks cost no mana, use no spell charges, and repeat at the hero's attack speed. Their damage grows every level: base damage + (level − 1) × damage per level, plus equipment bonuses. Q/W/E/R are four additional abilities with their own cooldowns and charges.

One target

Melee strikes need close range. Projectiles travel to one target. Neither becomes an area attack just because its effect looks wide. In player mode, aim at an object to target it, or aim at empty ground to swing or shoot into space.

An area on the map

Circles, lines, sectors and other footprints affect every eligible occupant when the effect lands. The area locks when cast. Moving into it before impact can get you hit; moving out can avoid it. Enemy damage, allied healing and structure protection still apply.

A visible warning

Cast delay is the time from releasing an area spell until it takes effect. The full footprint remains on the map during that delay, then flashes at impact. Every cast uses the same warnings and timing. Changing playback speed speeds up or slows down the battle and spell effects together.

Mana is paid when a cast is accepted. Basic attacks cost zero mana, as does Molten Fist. Other spells have their own mana costs. Invalid casts do not spend mana or charges; a valid shot into empty space does.

Charges

The number of casts held in reserve. You need at least one charge. Unlocking an ability grants its full charges. Respawning refills learned abilities. Locked abilities have no charges. Upgrading preserves spent charges and running cooldowns.

Cast cooldown

The minimum time between uses of the same action, even if charges remain. It is separate from the effect's cast delay and from its recharge timer.

Recharge cooldown

Charges refill one at a time. The timer starts with the first spent charge. Spending another does not restart it. When a charge returns, the next missing charge starts its own full recharge.

Try it: 3 charges · 2s cooldown · 12s recharge

Cast at 0, 2 and 4 seconds. Charges return at 12, 24 and 36 seconds if you stop casting.

Refill resources: Return to your own spawn room for fast health and mana recovery. Buy slow potions or instant elixirs in your own keep shop. Damage interrupts slow potion regeneration. Healing cannot revive a hero at zero HP.

PLAYER CONTROLS

Control your hero

Idle heroes automatically use their basic attack against the closest visible, attackable enemy nearby, including creeps, heroes, and exposed buildings. Melee heroes approach nearby enemies; ranged heroes acquire enemies within their attack range. Select an enemy or right-click it to make it take priority. Both melee and ranged heroes move toward that target until they reach their own basic-attack range, then stop and attack repeatedly. When the target dies or is lost in fog, they acquire another nearby visible enemy. A ground move order cancels the attack and takes priority over automatic acquisition. These rules also apply with zero mana or all four abilities on cooldown.

Spend points with Shift+Q/W/E/R, Shift-click on an ability, or its gold + button. The HUD shows ranks, locked abilities, and available points. Unlocks and upgrades are always explicit. Points and ranks survive death.

You control your four extra abilities; they only cast when you use them. Q/W/E/R cast immediately at your current valid target. Self actions always fire on key press and affect you. Melee actions also fire immediately: they strike a nearby target, or swing toward the pointer when there is none. For other abilities with no valid target, the key selects the ability and right-click casts at an object or the ground. Escape cancels the selection. With no ability selected, right-click moves or attacks. Click SHOP in the inventory, or press B, to open the full-screen item shop; click an item to buy it. Space pauses or resumes the battle, including while shopping. The HUD shows remaining charges and the wait until an empty action can be used again. A blue bar tracks progress toward the next charge.

BASIC HOST

BASIC observations

The base policy is a playable reference for all GotA host functions: drafting, ability upgrades and casting, last hits, objective pushing, enemy assessment, warning dodges, shopping, recovery, portals, and buyback. Calls are made when useful, so not every mechanic must occur in each match. Main decisions run every six ticks with bounded observation scans. Abilities and items require explicit policy commands; the engine never uses them automatically. Spell ranges and shapes have no host query and use a small table that must follow changes in content.nim.

Self data and visible objects are sampled for each decision and remain consistent during it, including after an action call. Spell queries read the pending casts, so a successful cast can append a spell during that decision. Object and spell indices are zero-based and may change next decision. Keep objectId(i) when tracking an object across decisions or calling attackTarget, rather than keeping its list index.

Observations are integers. worldScale = 60000 is the number of world units per tile, and tickRate = 24 is the number of simulation ticks per second. selfX, selfY, objectX(i), objectY(i), spellX(i), and spellY(i) use whole global tiles. Facing, speed, range, and velocity retain sub-tile precision in world units. The Y component of these APIs is the second horizontal map axis, not height.

levelAbility(slot) spends one point to unlock or upgrade a spell, returning 1 on success or 0 on rejection. Slots 0-3 correspond to Q/W/E/R. Points are earned at level 1 and each later hero level, and remain banked until spent. All four fully ranked abilities cost 15 points. Basic-attack damage continues scaling with hero level.

The first death takes 9 seconds to respawn, including the 1-second death animation. Each subsequent death adds 5 seconds, up to a total of 60 seconds. Counts belong to each hero and persist after respawning. BASIC decisions continue while dead.

Neutral camps

Fourteen clearings form seven mirrored camp pairs. Each half has three low camps (1–2 mobs), two medium camps (2–3), and two high camps (3–5). The map preset seed fixes each group's appearance and count. Groups share one chargen appearance; groups of two or more have a leader 35% larger, with double health, XP, and gold and 50% more damage.

Normal low/medium/high mobs have 100/180/300 HP, deal 8/14/20 melee damage, and award 20/35/50 XP and 10/20/30 last-hit gold. Camps attack heroes or lane creeps from either team within two tiles and line of sight of a living mob. Damaging a mob also engages its whole group. Idle attacks ignore resting camps; deliberate attacks, attack-move, and spells can engage them. Lane creeps can pull camps by approaching and fight back once the camp engages. A 12-tile leash returns survivors home, immune to damage and control until they arrive and fully heal. Dead members stay dead. Losing their aggressor also resets the camp, after three seconds if hidden.

After a full clear, the entire camp respawns after 60 seconds, waiting if any living hero is within ten tiles. Each respawn uses new object IDs. Neutral XP goes only to the last-hitting unit's team: nearby living heroes within six tiles on the same floor share it, reserving 15% for an eligible hero last hitter before sharing the other 85%. Only a hero last hitter receives gold.

Visible neutrals use objectKind(i) = 6, objectTeam(i) = 2, and tier 1–3 in objectClass(i). objectCamp(i) returns their zero-based camp index (-1 otherwise), objectLeader(i) identifies leaders, and objectReturning(i) identifies immune returning mobs. Existing health, target, movement, control, attack, and spell APIs apply. All dynamic information obeys terrain and brush LOS.

campCount(), campX(i), campY(i), and campTier(i) expose public static clearings in team-relative map coordinates. They never reveal hidden mob counts or respawn timers.

The puller policy copies the base policy and adds camp pulling. Healthy heroes approach a camp when a nearby allied wave is available, walk into aggro, then lead the neutrals through the wave. Offensive spells and items wait until the pull ends. Normal play resumes when creeps take over, danger appears, or the 15-second attempt expires. Attempts have a 20-second cooldown.

Each enemy creep death provides a shared pool of 15 XP to living heroes within six tiles on the same navigation floor, regardless of starting lane. A nearby hero who lands the last hit receives 15% of that pool first; the remaining 85% is split equally among all nearby heroes, including the last hitter. With three heroes, the last hitter earns 6.5 XP and each teammate earns 4.25 XP. Fractional XP carries forward between kills. Without an eligible hero last hitter, the full pool is shared equally. Hero last hits also award 15 gold; tower and creep last hits grant no gold to heroes.

Attack-move and the rusher policy

attackMove(x, y) follows a path toward a tile, fights enemies in the hero's normal acquisition range, and resumes afterward. It returns 1 when accepted and 0 when rejected. Orders are recorded in replays.

players/rusher.bas pushes all five heroes down mid together. When any pair of living allies is more than 10 tiles apart, the team regroups toward its center until the gap is at most 8 tiles. Otherwise it attacks visible, vulnerable enemies within 20 tiles or attack-moves through the middle toward the enemy god. Dead allies are ignored until they respawn. This policy uses basic attacks only.

Action feedback

lastActionError() reports your hero's latest submitted command error. Success clears it to NoActionError (0). Rejection preserves the command's 0 return value and records the first failed check. Read-only queries and automatic basic attacks do not overwrite it. Read the error immediately after the command; unlike sampled self data, it updates during a decision.

Terrain API

terrainKind(x, y), terrainWalkable(x, y), terrainHeight(x, y), and terrainWaterDepth(x, y) inspect terrain on selfLayer. Each also has an explicit layer form, such as terrainKindAt(x, y, layer). Coordinates are whole global tiles. Walkability is 1 or 0; height and water depth use eighths of a tile. Invalid coordinates, invalid layers, and absent tiles return zero. Static terrain remains observable through fog. Walkability also includes building footprints: enemy destruction is reflected only after your team sees it; unseen buildings retain their last known state.

Read-only constants expose mapWidth, mapHeight, mapLayers, GroundLayer, RedFortLayer, BlueFortLayer, and WaterLayer. Terrain kinds are TerrainNone, TerrainGrass, TerrainRoad, TerrainRock, TerrainTrees, TerrainMarsh, TerrainWall, and TerrainWater.

BASIC numbers and coordinates

BASIC uses Bassy with Fixxy Q16.16 decimals enabled. Globals and arrays retain fractional values across decisions. / performs decimal division; \ performs integer division. Decimal operands must fit -32768 through 32767.99998. Integer-only calculations retain the full signed 32-bit range. When converting large world-unit observations, divide them as integers first, for example (selfAttackRange \ 100) / (worldScale \ 100) in GotA.

and, or, xor, and not are bitwise. Comparisons produce -1 for true and 0 for false; conditions accept any nonzero number. Host flags and action results remain 1 or 0, so use flag = 0 instead of not flag to negate a host flag.

walkTo(x, y), attackMove(x, y), and castPoint(slot, x, y) accept fractional tile coordinates. For example, walkTo(selfX + 0.25, selfY - 0.25) selects a point a quarter tile from the current tile center. Integers continue to name tile centers. IDs, slots, indices, and terrain queries still require exact integers. Fractional arguments are recorded without truncation.
